FAFSA
The Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) is the form colleges use to decide your eligibility for federal grants, work-study, and loans. Many states and colleges use it for their own aid too. It is free to file, so never pay a company to do it. It typically opens each fall, so check studentaid.gov for this year's dates and deadlines.
Grants vs. loans
Grants and scholarships are gift aid you do not repay. Loans must be repaid with interest. Work-study is money you earn through a part-time job. Use gift aid first, then federal loans, and consider private loans last because they have fewer protections.
Scholarships
Scholarships can be based on merit, financial need, background, or your intended major. Apply to many, including small local awards, which often have fewer applicants. Track deadlines, and never pay a fee to apply.
Student debt
Borrow only what you need. Interest can build while you are in school, so a loan grows before your first payment. A common rule of thumb is to keep your total borrowing below your expected first-year salary. Use the Loans & ROI tab to see what a monthly payment would feel like.
Choosing affordable colleges
Compare the net price, which is the cost after grants and scholarships, instead of the sticker price. Every college has a net price calculator on its website. In-state public colleges and community college transfer paths can cost much less. Graduation rates matter too, since extra years add to the bill.

These are estimates for planning, not offers of aid. Real aid depends on each college and your FAFSA results.

Why Open Path Labs exists.

Money decisions like paying for college, borrowing, and saving shape people's futures, but clear, practical guidance can be hard to find.

We build free tools, lessons, and study resources that let people try the numbers before they decide, and we share them with schools, nonprofits, and community groups.

Mission

Make financial education more accessible through technology, educational resources, and community partnerships.

Vision

A world where anyone can make a financial decision with the information they need.
